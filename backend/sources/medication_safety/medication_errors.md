# Summary

## Medication safety

Medicines can cause harmful reactions if not used correctly. To help prevent errors, know the names of your medicines and confirm that the pharmacy supplied the intended medicine. Understand how often and how long a medicine was prescribed to be taken; ask the prescriber or pharmacist if unsure.

Keep a current list of prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, supplements, and herbs, as well as allergies or medicines that caused problems. Bring the list to health care visits. Read medicine labels each time, and take special care when medicines are given to children.

Ask a health care provider or pharmacist about a medicine's purpose, possible side effects, what to do about side effects, when to stop it, interactions with other medicines or supplements, and whether foods or alcohol should be avoided. This information does not replace instructions from the person's prescriber or pharmacist.

## Reading labels and following instructions

Read the label each time, including the active ingredients, purpose, warnings, directions, and storage information. Check that the medicine is the intended product. Follow the label and the prescriber's instructions; ask the prescriber or pharmacist before changing how a prescribed medicine is used or stopping it. Different products can contain the same active ingredient, and labels can change.

## Interactions and questions

Interactions can involve other medicines, vitamins, herbal or other supplements, food, drinks, or health conditions. Keep an up-to-date list and share it with the pharmacist and health professionals, including allergies and past reactions. Useful questions include: What is this medicine for? How and for how long should I use it? What should I do if I miss a dose? What side effects need attention? Could it interact with my other medicines, supplements, food, or drink? How should it be stored, and what should I do with unused medicine?

## Safe storage

Keep medicines in their original labeled containers where possible, stored according to the label or pharmacist's instructions, and out of sight and reach of children. Heat, light, air, and moisture can damage some medicines. Do not assume a bathroom cabinet is suitable; check product-specific storage directions. Ask a pharmacist how to dispose of unused or expired medicines because local disposal guidance differs.
