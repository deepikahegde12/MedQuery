# Summary

## What is the common cold?

The common cold is a mild infection of your upper respiratory tract (which includes your nose and throat). More than 200 different viruses can cause a cold, but rhinoviruses are the most common type.

## What are the symptoms of the common cold?

The symptoms of a common cold usually include sneezing, a stuffy or runny nose, sore throat, coughing, and headache. Symptoms usually start a few days after infection. Some symptoms can last for 10–14 days.

## When should someone contact a health care provider?

Contact your provider if you or your child have symptoms that concern you, such as trouble breathing or fast breathing; dehydration; fever lasting longer than four days; symptoms lasting more than ten days without improvement; symptoms that improve and then return or worsen; or worsening chronic medical conditions.

Cold and cough medicines may be inappropriate for some children. Children and teens should not take aspirin for a cold, and a child's health care provider should be consulted before giving cold or cough medicines. Read labels carefully to avoid taking the same pain-relieving ingredient in more than one medicine; ask a provider or pharmacist if unsure. Antibiotics do not treat viral infections such as colds.
