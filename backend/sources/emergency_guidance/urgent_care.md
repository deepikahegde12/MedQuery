# General emergency warning signs and urgent care

## Scope

This is general information, not a comprehensive triage protocol. It cannot determine whether a person's condition is an emergency. When in doubt about immediate danger, contact local emergency medical services or go to an emergency department. Emergency telephone numbers and service arrangements vary by location.

## Warning signs

Examples of signs that may need immediate emergency assessment include severe difficulty breathing; unresponsiveness, fainting, or difficulty waking; new confusion or inability to speak; chest pain or pressure; choking; bleeding that will not stop; coughing or vomiting blood; a severe head or spine injury; severe abdominal pain; or a sudden severe allergic reaction with breathing difficulty or swelling of the mouth or throat. These examples are not a complete list.

Sudden weakness or numbness, especially on one side, new trouble speaking, seeing, or walking, or a sudden severe headache can also signal a time-critical problem. Even if symptoms improve, seek emergency help promptly.

## What to do

If a person appears to be in immediate danger, contact the emergency service for the place where they are. Follow the call handler's instructions. Do not delay to use an online symptom checker. If symptoms are serious but not immediately life-threatening, contact a local urgent-care service or qualified health professional for advice. Do not drive yourself if you feel faint, confused, severely short of breath, or otherwise unsafe to drive.

## Mental health crisis

If someone may harm themselves or another person, or cannot stay safe, treat this as urgent. Stay with them if it is safe to do so, reduce immediate access to hazards where this can be done safely, and contact local emergency services or a crisis service available in that location. A U.S.-specific hotline is not a universal service.
