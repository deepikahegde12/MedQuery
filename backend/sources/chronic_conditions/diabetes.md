# Summary

## What is diabetes?

Diabetes, also known as diabetes mellitus, is a disease in which blood glucose (blood sugar) levels are too high. Glucose is the body's main source of energy. Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas that helps move glucose from the bloodstream into cells. In diabetes, the body may not make insulin, may not use insulin as well as it should, or both. Over time, high blood glucose levels can lead to serious health conditions.

## What are the types of diabetes?

Type 1 diabetes involves little or no insulin production. Type 2 diabetes involves cells that do not respond well to insulin. Gestational diabetes develops during pregnancy when the body cannot make the extra insulin needed.

## What are possible symptoms?

Symptoms may include feeling very thirsty or hungry, urinating more often, fatigue, blurry vision, numbness or tingling in the hands or feet, sores that do not heal, and unintentional weight loss. Symptoms vary by type: type 1 symptoms usually come on quickly and can be severe; type 2 symptoms often develop slowly and may be mild; gestational diabetes often has no symptoms.

## How is diabetes diagnosed?

A health care provider uses one or more glucose blood tests, which may include an A1C test. Symptoms alone do not establish a diagnosis.
